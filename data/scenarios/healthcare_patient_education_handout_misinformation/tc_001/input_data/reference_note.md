# Statin Therapy — Clinical Reference Summary

## Overview
HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins) are the cornerstone of LDL-lowering therapy.
Simvastatin is a CYP3A4-metabolized statin commonly prescribed for hyperlipidemia.

## Mechanism
Simvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, reducing hepatic cholesterol synthesis and
upregulating LDL receptors, resulting in lower plasma LDL-C levels.

## Standard Dosing
- Simvastatin 20–40 mg orally once daily in the evening.
- Lisinopril 10 mg orally once daily.
- Aspirin 81 mg orally once daily.

## Key Drug Interactions
- CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, itraconazole) markedly increase simvastatin
  plasma levels and raise the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. Concomitant use
  should be avoided.
- Gemfibrozil increases simvastatin AUC; combination is contraindicated.

## Monitoring
- Liver function tests at baseline and if symptomatic.
- CK if myalgias develop.
- Lipid panel at 4–12 weeks after initiation to assess response.

## Patient Counseling Points
- Take simvastatin in the evening for maximum LDL-lowering efficacy.
- Report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness promptly.
- Maintain a low-saturated-fat diet.
- Adhere to regular follow-up appointments for lipid monitoring.

## Notes
This reference note does not contain any dietary restriction regarding grapefruit or
citrus fruits. Any dietary guidance must be derived independently by the reviewing
pharmacist or prescriber and documented separately.
