PERSONAL STATEMENT:
There is a peculiar pleasure in sitting with a problem that refuses to resolve — not the frustration of incomprehension, but something closer to the satisfaction of recognizing that the resistance itself is informative. I have spent much of high school cultivating that pleasure deliberately, seeking out questions whose surfaces are deceptive and whose depths reward sustained engagement.

My interest in philosophy began with a summer program that introduced me to epistemological debates I had not known existed. I arrived expecting abstract puzzles; I departed with something more unsettling — a conviction that the frameworks I used to evaluate claims were themselves claims, and therefore subject to the same scrutiny I applied outward. It is a vertiginous realization, and one I have found enormously productive.

In my podcast, I have tried to bring that same interrogative spirit to conversations with peers and local professionals about how they navigate uncertainty in their own domains — from architecture to medicine to law. The recurring theme has been that expertise is less about possessing answers than about knowing which questions to distrust.

I am applying to this program because I believe rigorous intellectual community is a prerequisite for the kind of thinking I want to do. I do not want an audience; I want interlocutors.
